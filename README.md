## What kind of dog bed does your dog actually need?

The honest answer: most dogs need one of three types, and the fancier categories are situational.

**Orthopedic beds** use a solid foam base (often egg-crate polyurethane or memory foam) instead of loose polyester fill. These make sense for senior dogs, large and giant breeds prone to hip problems, dogs recovering from surgery, and honestly any dog that circles for five minutes before settling. The reason is mechanical — a foam base supports weight evenly, while cheap stuffing flattens into a pancake within months. Forbes Vetted's review team made exactly this point about budget beds: many "value" options rely on polyester fill that compresses quickly, which is why the ones with a dense egg-crate base hold up.

**Bolster beds** (raised edges around the perimeter) suit dogs that like resting their head on something — a lot of terriers, beagles, and anxious sleepers fall in this group. Many orthopedic models combine both: foam base plus a bolster, which is the configuration most reviewers keep recommending as the default.

**Chew-proof / durable beds** are for the destroyers. This is the one category where paying more genuinely buys something: Orvis's ToughChew line, for example, is the only cushioned bed with a lifetime chew guarantee, per Reviewed's testing update. If your dog shreds everything, a "tough fabric" claim without a warranty isn't worth much — look for one that actually backs it.

Everything else — heated beds, cooling mats, "calming donut" beds — is a nice-to-have. A cooling mat helps double-coated dogs in summer; a heated bed helps thin-coated seniors in winter. The calming donut? Some dogs love it, some ignore it completely. Don't pay a premium on faith.
